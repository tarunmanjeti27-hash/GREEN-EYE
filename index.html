"The Cancer of Sugarcane"

Sugarcane Red Rot Disease

Pathogen: Colletotrichum falcatum Went (Teleomorph: Glomerella tucumanensis)

Disease Overview & Symptoms

Red Rot is the most catastrophic disease of sugarcane in tropical and subtropical regions. First symptoms on leaves appear as minute red lesions on the midrib that elongate and develop characteristic white centers with dark red/brown margins. As infection advances systemically, leaves wither from tip to base, canes lose weight, shrivel, and longitudinal splitting reveals reddened pith tissues with transverse white patches and an alcoholic fermentative odor.

Transmission & Environmental Triggers

  • Primary Source: Infected seed cane sets and dormant mycelium in soil or ratoon stubble.
  • Secondary Spread: Rain splash, irrigation runoff, boring insects (e.g. stalk borers).
  • Favorable Conditions: Waterlogged soil, high relative humidity (>85%), temperature 28°C–32°C, susceptible monocultures.

Integrated Management Protocol

1
Clean Seed Selection

Plant certified disease-free sets. Treat sets with hot water at 52°C for 30 minutes or hot moist air at 54°C for 2.5 hours.

2
Chemical Dip & Spray

Soak sets in Carbendazim 50% WP (1g/L) for 15 minutes before sowing. Apply foliar spray of Thiophanate-methyl if midrib lesions appear.

3
Resistant Varieties

Cultivate proven tolerant cultivars like Co 86032, Co 0238 (with monitoring), and avoid continuous ratoon in infected plots.

Potyviridae Complex

Sugarcane Mosaic Virus (SCMV)

Pathogen: Sugarcane mosaic virus (SCMV / SrMV) / Genus: Potyvirus

Disease Overview & Symptoms

Sugarcane Mosaic is a worldwide viral disease characterized by foliar chlorosis. Leaves exhibit distinctive mottled patterns of contrasting light green, yellowish islands against normal dark green tissue. The mosaic pattern is most evident on young expanding leaves near the spindle. Severe viral strains cause stunting, shortened internodes, and diminished juice sucrose concentration.

Transmission Vectors

  • Insect Vectors: Non-persistently vectored by aphids including Rhopalosiphum maidis (Corn leaf aphid) and Aphis craccivora.
  • Mechanical Spread: Cutting knives, harvest harvesters, and vegetative propagation of infected seed cane.
  • Alternative Hosts: Maize (Zea mays), sorghum, and wild grasses (Sorghum halepense).

Integrated Management Protocol

1
Rogueing & Sanitation

Regularly walk fields during early tillering. Dig out and burn all mosaic-affected clumps immediately.

2
Vector Suppression

Control aphid colonies using Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3ml/L or systemic bio-neem oil extracts.

3
Meristem Tissue Culture

Establish seed cane nurseries using virus-indexed apical meristem tissue cultured planting materials.

Foliar Fungal Epiphytotic

Sugarcane Rust (Common & Orange)

Pathogen: Puccinia melanocephala (Common Rust) & Puccinia kuehnii (Orange Rust)

Disease Overview & Symptoms

Sugarcane Rust affects the photosynthetic capacity of mature and young leaves. It manifests as small, elongated chlorotic flecks that rapidly expand into cinnamon-brown to orange-brown pustules (uredinia) parallel to the leaf venation. The epidermis ruptures, discharging massive amounts of powdery rust spores. Severe coalescence results in premature leaf desiccation, premature leaf drop, and poor stalk girth.

Weather Triggers & Epidemiology

  • Environmental Preference: Moderate temperatures (20°C–26°C), prolonged dew periods (>8 hours), and cloudy overcast days.
  • Dispersal: High-altitude wind currents carry urediniospores across hundreds of kilometers.

Integrated Management Protocol

1
Protective Fungicide Spray

Apply Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5g/L or Pyraclostrobin + Epoxiconazole at first sign of flecking.

2
Nutritional Balance

Avoid excessive nitrogenous fertilization which promotes succulent susceptible tissues; boost Potassium (K) levels.

3
Canopy Aeration

Maintain recommended row spacing (120 cm to 150 cm) to encourage air circulation and rapid leaf surface drying.

Foliar Phloem Disorder

Sugarcane Yellow Leaf Disease (SCYLD)

Pathogen: Sugarcane yellow leaf virus (SCYLV) / Genus: Polerovirus

Disease Overview & Symptoms

Yellow Leaf Disease causes intense midrib chlorosis. In symptomatic plants, the lower surface of the midrib on leaves 3 to 6 develops an intense golden-yellow coloration while the leaf blade remains green initially. Subsequently, yellowing spreads laterally into the lamina, followed by pinkish/purple discoloration and distal necrosis. The virus damages phloem vessels, obstructing sucrose translocation to the root system and cane stalks.

Transmission & Latency

  • Vectors: Semi-persistently transmitted by the sugarcane aphid (Melanaphis sacchari).
  • Silent Infections: Can remain asymptomatic under optimal irrigation, becoming pronounced during water stress or crop maturity.

Integrated Management Protocol

1
Certified Seed Cane Nursery

Employ serological (TBIA) or molecular (RT-PCR) tested disease-free seed sets for new plantation establishment.

2
Aphid Colony Control

Monitor underleaf surfaces for Melanaphis sacchari colonies and spray systemic insecticide Thiamethoxam 25% WG @ 0.3g/L.

3
Soil Moisture Management

Mitigate moisture stress through drip irrigation to prevent symptom aggravation during sucrose accumulation stages.

Agronomic Standard

Healthy Sugarcane Foliage Benchmark

Crop: Saccharum officinarum L. / Family: Poaceae

Physical Health Characteristics

Healthy sugarcane leaves display uniform deep emerald green pigmentation across the entire blade with clean, sturdy, white-to-light-green midribs devoid of discoloration, chlorosis, or necrotic pustules. Leaf margins are smooth and intact with erect to semi-erect phyllotaxy, ensuring optimal light interception and photosynthetic conversion.

Key Productivity Parameters

  • Leaf Area Index (LAI): 4.5 – 6.0 during peak grand growth stage.
  • SPAD Chlorophyll Reading: 42 – 48 SPAD units under balanced nutritional status.
  • Canopy Interception: >90% photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) absorbed.

Best Maintenance Practices

1
Balanced N-P-K Nutrition

Apply recommended fertilizer doses (250:100:120 kg/ha N:P:K) split across tillering and elongation phases.

2
Detrashing Practices

Remove dried lower leaves (detrashing) at 5th and 7th month to minimize humidity and suppress scale insects.

3
Prophylactic Bio-Sprays

Spray fermented pseudomonas or mycorrhizal inoculants to reinforce foliar cuticle defense mechanisms.
